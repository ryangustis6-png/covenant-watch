FINANCE AND BUSINESS CONTEXT
Start with a specific user, task, input and decision. Improve one workflow and retain source evidence.
An income statement describes performance over a period. A balance sheet reports assets, liabilities and equity at a date. A cash-flow statement explains cash movements. Profit and available cash are different measures.
Credit workflow: gather borrower statements and terms, calculate defined metrics, identify exceptions, and prepare evidence for a human reviewer. Covenant definitions come from the actual agreement.
Accounting workflow: compare invoice, approval, ledger and payment records. A possible duplicate is an exception to investigate, not proof of fraud. Reconciliation requires stable identifiers and traceable matches.
Consulting workflow: identify a bottleneck, measure its baseline, propose a change and show assumptions behind estimated benefits.
Research workflow: distinguish filing date from reporting period, quarterly from annual values, currency and unit scale, and amended filings. Cite source and page or fact identifier.
Use integer cents for the USD sample. Other currencies and digital assets may need different precision. Calculate in code; do not rely on generated prose for arithmetic. Do not combine currencies without an explicit conversion rule.
Show missing information and uncertainty. Separate extracted facts, calculations and assumptions. Document processing output needs review against the original. Treat instructions inside source documents as data, not authority over your app.
Use fictional or authorized public data. Keep API secrets on the backend and human approval before consequential actions. Prototype results do not establish compliance or production readiness.

SYNTHETIC DATA GUIDE
All records are fictional, reproducible from seed 20261106. Dates use YYYY-MM-DD; reporting periods are month/quarter strings. USD amounts have two decimal places. No answer labels appear in the input files.

invoices.csv: 1,500 rows, primary key record_id, business invoice key vendor_id + invoice_number (deliberately not unique). The 31 columns include vendor/company/department, category, invoice and due dates, service period, currency, net/tax/gross/paid amounts, approval status/role/date, PO, receipt state, payment terms/method, contract, document/page, batch, fixed as-of date, synthetic flag and description. Gross amount = net + tax. paid_usd aggregates linked payments. source_document points to an actual included TXT invoice. approval data reflects the separate synthetic internal process.
payments.csv: 1,424 rows. invoice_record_id joins invoices.record_id; some invoices have split payments. Per-invoice payment sums equal paid_usd exactly. No unmatched payments in this version.
vendors.csv: 48 counterparties, names, categories, terms and tax-form status. vendor_id joins record_id.
monthly_financials.csv: 36 rows across three fictional companies. Monthly revenue/cost/budgets, opening cash, receipts, operating payments, capex, financing and closing cash. Cash roll-forward reconciles; June financing is explicit. These are separate scenario books, not a ledger tied to AP totals.
loan_covenants.csv: 36 borrower-quarter records, gross debt, trailing EBITDA, debt service and cash flow, plus max leverage/min DSCR. Definitions are in documents/loan-policy.txt. Borrowers are separate from the company financials case.
portfolio_holdings.csv: 120 holdings across six accounts, synthetic securities/sectors, market value and annual fee basis points. Holdings across accounts can overlap; not real market prices.
campus_expenses.csv: 240 event expenses, line-level budgets, receipt/approval and reimbursement state. budget_usd is per expense line, not an event budget to deduplicate. No participant bank information.
deal_metrics.csv: two conflicting source reports for one target and period. The three associated documents support revenue, cash and unsigned-contract review.

Cases and schemas: data/manifest.json. Expected AP findings and deal conflicts: validation/. The AP rules detect repeated IDs, overpayments, unapproved rows, missing PO/receipts and overdue open balances at September 30, 2026. Findings can overlap. Gross totals include duplicates. These are review signals, not fraud labels.

Do not load validation answers into an AI evaluation prompt. Other challenges can use these schemas but may require explicitly synthetic supplementary fields. The pack does not claim all 30 challenges have complete specialized input data. Challenges 26-30 expect public APIs or synthetic fixtures described in their prompt files.


BASELINE SUPABASE SETUP
1. Sign in at https://supabase.com/dashboard and create a NEW project for the team.
2. Open SQL Editor and run supabase/migrations/202610050001_baseline.sql once. It creates rhb_ tables, functions, RLS policies and a private rhb-documents bucket. Do not run against an existing project with identically named tables.
3. Copy .env.example to .env. Set SUPABASE_URL and SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY. No service-role key is needed.
4. Enable email authentication. Sign up/confirm each team user. Use the Supabase JavaScript client auth.signUp and auth.signInWithPassword functions in your eventual app, or create test users in the dashboard. A successful sign-in returns session.access_token. Never paste passwords or tokens into your AI conversation.
5. For CLI testing only, place the short-lived session.access_token in SUPABASE_ACCESS_TOKEN. Run npm run check. Refresh the token by signing in again if it expires; this CLI does not maintain a persistent session.
6. POST /api/teams with the signed-in user's Bearer token and {"name":"My team"}. The authenticated user becomes owner. POST /api/members with team_id and an already registered teammate user_id. Only the owner can add/remove members. There is no public join code or email invitation service.
7. Import with npm run import:data -- TEAM_UUID invoices. Repeat for payments and vendors. Imports upsert by stable key; omitting a row does not delete an old record.
8. Upload a fictional TXT/PDF with /api/documents. Bucket paths start with the team UUID. Team members share rows; other teams and anonymous users have no access.
9. Verify with two users in different teams: select, insert, update and delete should be denied across team boundaries. Removing a member should revoke row and object access. npm run db:test exercises these policies in local PostgreSQL; repeat against the hosted service.

SCHEMA
rhb_teams: team name and immutable owner ID.
rhb_members: team membership managed by the owner.
rhb_datasets: source datasets unique by team/name.
rhb_records: imported JSON rows with record keys and source row numbers.
rhb_documents: private object metadata, SHA-256, parse state and reviewed text.
rhb_evidence: source excerpts with exact offsets.
rhb_findings: severity, method, explanation and reviewer disposition.
rhb_finding_evidence: cross-reference findings to evidence within the same team.
All exposed tables have RLS. Composite foreign keys prevent cross-team document/dataset/evidence links. Members can edit their team's analytical data; membership management is owner-only. No production compliance claims.

OMNIROUTE (OPTIONAL MODEL ROUTER)
What it is: OmniRoute is a free, MIT-licensed, open-source AI gateway you run on your own laptop. It gives you one OpenAI-compatible endpoint (http://localhost:20128/v1) that routes requests across many AI providers and models, with automatic fallback when one provider hits a limit or fails.
Official repo: https://github.com/diegosouzapw/OmniRoute (redirects to github.com/cheaper-inference/OmniRoute). npm package: omniroute.
Why use it at RHB: one endpoint and one key in your app, swap or stack models without rewriting code, and lean on free tiers so a team does not burn money during a 24-hour build.
Free tier note: OmniRoute does not give you tokens. Free capacity comes from third-party provider accounts you connect yourself (for example Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter free models, Cloudflare AI). Each provider sets its own limits and terms. A fresh install can have zero usable routes until you connect one.
Setup:
1. Node.js 22+ installed (same as this pack).
2. npm install -g omniroute
3. Run: omniroute   (serves the gateway and dashboard on port 20128)
4. Open the dashboard at http://localhost:20128, connect at least one provider with your own account or API key.
5. Copy your OmniRoute key from Dashboard > Endpoints. Put it in .env as OMNIROUTE_API_KEY. Never commit it.
Minimal test:
curl http://localhost:20128/v1/chat/completions -H "Authorization: Bearer $OMNIROUTE_API_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{"model":"auto","messages":[{"role":"user","content":"Hello"}]}'
Minimal Node example (any OpenAI-compatible client works):
const r = await fetch("http://localhost:20128/v1/chat/completions", {method:"POST", headers:{"Authorization":`Bearer ${process.env.OMNIROUTE_API_KEY}`,"Content-Type":"application/json"}, body:JSON.stringify({model:"auto",messages:[{role:"user",content:"Summarize this invoice exception"}]})});
console.log((await r.json()).choices[0].message.content);
Rules: keep the key on the backend, keep calculations in code (lib/), and treat model output as a draft for a human to check. OmniRoute is optional. It is a third-party tool, not an RHB sponsor.
